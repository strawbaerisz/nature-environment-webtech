Nature Facts

Learn more about the environments featured in our gallery.

Forests are vital because they regulate the Earth’s climate, sustain biodiversity, and provide resources for human survival. The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) notes that forests absorb about 2.6 billion tons of carbon dioxide annually, acting as powerful carbon sinks that slow global warming. The World Wildlife Fund (WWF) emphasizes that forests are home to over 80% of terrestrial species, making them irreplaceable habitats for countless plants and animals. Without forests, ecosystems would collapse, and species extinction rates would accelerate dramatically.

Beyond ecology, forests directly support human life and well-being. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) reports that around 1.6 billion people depend on forests for food, medicine, fuel, and shelter. Forests also regulate the water cycle, with nearly half of the world’s largest cities relying on forested watersheds for clean drinking water. According to UNEP, spending time in forests improves cardiovascular health, reduces stress, and lowers risks of respiratory diseases, while deforestation increases the risk of zoonotic diseases that can lead to pandemics. In short, forests are lifelines for biodiversity, climate stability, human livelihoods, and global health.

Oceans are fundamental to life on Earth because they regulate the climate, produce oxygen, and sustain biodiversity. The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) explains that oceans absorb about 30% of carbon dioxide emissions and capture 90% of excess heat from the atmosphere, making them the planet’s largest climate regulator. They also produce over 50% of the world’s oxygen through phytoplankton, tiny marine plants that act as the lungs of the Earth. According to the World Wildlife Fund (WWF), oceans are home to nearly 80% of all life on Earth, from coral reefs to deep-sea ecosystems, making them biodiversity hotspots that are critical for ecological balance.

Beyond environmental functions, oceans directly support human survival and economies. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) reports that more than 3 billion people depend on oceans for their livelihoods, particularly through fisheries and coastal industries. Oceans also drive the global water cycle, supplying rainfall and moderating weather patterns, which is vital for agriculture and freshwater systems. UNEP highlights that healthy oceans provide food security, medicine, and coastal protection, while degraded oceans increase risks such as rising sea levels and stronger storms.

We can help the environment by making conscious choices that reduce waste, conserve resources, and restore ecosystems. The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) highlights that restoring degraded lands and forests is critical for biodiversity and climate resilience, while everyday actions like eating more plant-based meals, biking instead of driving, and switching to energy-efficient appliances can significantly lower carbon footprints. The World Wildlife Fund (WWF) stresses that cutting single-use plastics and supporting recycling initiatives are vital to reducing pollution, especially in oceans where millions of tons of plastic accumulate each year.

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), supporting local and sustainable practices—such as buying local produce, conserving water, and planting native trees—strengthens communities while protecting ecosystems. In short, small daily actions combined with community efforts can collectively safeguard the planet for future generations.
